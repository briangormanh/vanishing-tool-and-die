The bottleneck

A factory boom needs toolmakers to build the tools

Before a plant can stamp a part or mold a component, someone has to build the die or the mold first. That is the toolmaker's job, and the country is trying to reshore faster than it is replacing them.

How we did this

Methods, sources & the honest caveats

What we counted

We looked at "tool and die makers," the skilled workers who build the dies, molds, jigs, and fixtures that factories use to shape metal and plastic parts. The worker counts and wages come from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. The shop counts (the number of tool, die, and mold businesses) come from the Census Bureau. Worker ages come from a separate Census survey of individual workers.

How we measured the decade change

We compared the government's 2014 estimate of workers to its 2024 estimate for the country and for each state. These are point-in-time estimates, not an official change statistic, so treat the decade figures as a strong signal of direction rather than an exact count.

How we estimated retirements

Nationally, about 55% of tool & die makers are 55 or older. To estimate how many might retire this decade in each state, we applied that national share to each state's current workforce. This assumes every state has a similar age mix, which is a reasonable national-structure estimate, not a measured state-by-state age count.

What "concentration" means

The concentration figure shows how common the trade is in a state compared with the national average. A value of 1.0 is exactly average. A value of 3.0 means the trade is three times as concentrated there. It is a way to see where tool & die work is a bigger part of the local economy.

Caveats worth stating plainly

  • The "likely to retire" figures apply a national age pattern to each state, so they are estimates, not head counts.
  • The 2014-versus-2024 comparison uses point-in-time estimates and is not an official change measure.
  • Census shop and worker-age counts are from 2023 and are lightly adjusted to protect individual privacy.
  • Some smaller states are not published and appear as "not reported."
